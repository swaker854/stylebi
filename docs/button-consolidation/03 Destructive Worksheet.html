Destructive Action Worksheet

The dev's label sweep found 59 destructive-labelled buttons against 1 styled as danger. But a destructive label isn't a destructive action — so the rubric adds a second cut before anything turns red. The real problem here isn't too much alarm; it's that destructive actions are invisible.

source: button-inventory (label sweep) 59 destructive labels · 1 styled danger rule: R3 refined

1 · The finding

59vs1
destructive-labelled buttons vs. buttons actually styled as danger

Destructive actions today carry the same visual weight as benign ones — "Delete" renders identically to "Add field" (both btn-default → ghost). The risk isn't scary red everywhere; it's the opposite: users can't tell a destructive action apart from a routine one. Danger styling restores a distinction that's currently missing.

2 · The cut — label ≠ action

Half the "destructive" labels are reversible resets. Applying danger to those would cry wolf. Split first:

3 · R3, refined

A destructive label is a candidate, not a verdict. Two cuts, in order:

R3Destructive label → variant
1Reversible? Clears/resets a field, filter, or selection — nothing persistent lost.Clear
2Destroys data, and is the affirmative action of a confirm dialog?Delete
3Destroys data, inline / in a row / action bar among other actions?Remove

Lint note: never auto-apply .is-danger from a label. The sweep flags candidates; a reviewer applies cut #1. Otherwise 30 harmless resets turn red.

Step 2 fires nowhere in this codebase

Dev inspection confirmed it: across all 29 destructive sites, not one is the affirmative CTA of a confirm dialog. Every destructive action is a secondary or inline action sitting beside an OK/Cancel or among other row actions. So .btn-primary.is-danger is unused here — the rule keeps it for completeness, but the codebase only needs .btn-ghost.is-danger.

4 · Resolution — all 29 destructive

Dev inspection settled the two former footer cases — neither is the dialog's affirmative action. edit-join-dialog:143 (Remove) sits beside its own OK + Cancel; task-action-pane:87 (Delete) isn't a modal footer at all but a custom action bar among Add/Copy/Edit/Save. Both are destructive secondaries. The "Confirm" bucket is empty.

The one class-change beyond the codemod: analyze-mv-dialog:54

Of all 29, exactly one destructive action is currently a full btn-primary — "Delete" in analyze-mv, one of three competing primaries (see Resolution Worksheet §2). It needs an explicit btn-primary → btn-ghost swap plus .is-danger; the other 28 already become ghost via the default/light codemod and just gain .is-danger. A destructive action should never wear the primary CTA.
